Professionally, I am not a developer. DevOps does not mean I develop! It means I work with development. At large corporations and companies, I work between development and operations, in middleware.

It requires me to know Windows batch scripting, PowerShell on Windows, Bash on Linux, and shell scripting on Linux. And yes, Bash—the **Bourne Again Shell**—and the Bourne shell are different.

It never once required Python, and it shouldn’t have to. Though we all know Python and keep it quiet! Stay out of my hobby.

Next: Jinja and Terraform. Open-source versus closed-source tools. SaltStack versus Ansible. Industry scripting and query languages, like SQL. However, I have never personally met a DevOps engineer who worked in SQL outside SIEM analysis. Granted, I know it. You all might as well make me a business analyst, because I had to learn Power BI, too.

Put that in your pipe and smoke it.

Now, what’s with the industry right now, and why are infrastructure engineers being treated as developers?

Man, all my professional work was behind NDAs and noncompetes. I can’t showcase my work. You all might already be using my code as it stands today. Who cares now?

The fight used to be, “Oh, you don’t know Ansible.”

Oh, wait—I don’t know your GUI. Sorry, Red Hat team. I know a CLI command center. It’s a black box. What’s harder?

That was a few years ago.

Next! “You don’t know Fabric.”

Bitch, I know Palantir and Fortinet Security Fabric. What’s the matter? Can’t figure out how to make a VRF without the Easy-Zone Button?

Fair enough, I haven’t configured one through code or a GUI in a bit. But a quick review of the help docs may come in handy, HR hiring rep. Sorry, I’m only 75% photographic.

Next! I forgot in my frustration.

Lastly, once I’m doing your Power BI reports while maintaining your database instances and migrating them over to MongoDB to avoid impending EOL and EOS dates—end of life and end of support—I’m also busy running through Elastic or your SIEM, maybe Splunk.

I’m sifting through hundreds of databases and billions of packets for the day, with two years of backlog, just to find an **untagged server**, because your previous SOC team wasn’t even SOC- or NIST-compliant and had no tags!

To be clear: that scenario is imagined. I’m not describing a former employer. But I know YOU exist out there. One of you didn’t tag your servers in an environment 100,000 resource groups strong and bigger than the railroad.

*Hint, hint.*

Ahh, lastly! I remembered.

I have ADHD. I’m tired of being mapped through Datadog’s RUM by armchair psychologists who CODE!

I’m close to a psychology degree, missing only my core, non-industry classes. Put me on THAT.

I’m tired of assumptions about my work when I know I’m being watched live and the Hawthorne effect kicks in. Imagine someone determining the scope of your ADHD-related memory issues from data while you know you’re being watched.

Think of it like this: imagine a road-test instructor for your JOB, 24/7.

A good senior would watch over your shoulder just before you hit [ENTER]. Sometimes, they’d let you hit [ENTER] wrong so you could learn when it wasn’t production. If it was production, mine would have let it go anyway and made me fix my fuckups.

That alone scared me enough to add a final mental review before hitting Enter at work. Not on my home device, by the way.

So now we all have to lie on that hiring document and say “no disability,” no matter what?

Last bit: I call my home lab **“a crash box.”**

I hope nobody has based anything about my professional life on what I do at home.
