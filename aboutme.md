I'm Rali0s, a Systems Engineer with a focus on site reliability, cloud infrastructure, DevOps, automation, and security information and event management (SIEM). My interests center on how systems behave in practice: how they are deployed, monitored, maintained, and recovered when something goes wrong.

### Engineering with operations in mind

I approach technology from the operations side. A working application is one part of a larger system that needs dependable infrastructure, useful monitoring, clear troubleshooting paths, and a manageable deployment process. I'm interested in the work that connects those pieces and makes a service easier to support throughout its life.

Automation is a natural part of that approach. It offers a way to reduce repetitive work, make procedures more consistent, and give people more time to investigate problems that require judgment. Reliability and security matter throughout that process, from understanding a failure to knowing whether a fix actually restored service.

### Why I code

I code as a hobby. My professional identity is in systems engineering and operations; software development is an area I explore to build useful tools, test ideas, and better understand the applications those systems support.

Working on my own projects helps me collaborate more effectively with development teams. It gives me a closer view of implementation choices, dependencies, debugging, and the tradeoffs involved in getting an idea into a working application. That perspective helps me connect development decisions with operational needs such as deployment, observability, maintenance, and recovery.

### What you'll find here

This blog is a record of that ongoing work: hobby projects, experiments, technical notes, and changelogs. Some projects are early explorations; others are moving through testing or hardware QA. I document their current stages so the distinction between an idea, a working prototype, and a release stays clear.

The projects span different interests, but they share a practical motivation: learn by building, understand how the pieces fit together, and carry those lessons back into better systems work.
